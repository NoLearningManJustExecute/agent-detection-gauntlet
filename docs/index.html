AI-agent detection, measured

Behavior survives what blinds keywords.

14 detection rules, frozen before scoring, run against a live agent's real attacks. Then the same attacks with their characters changed, re-executed to see what still works and what still gets caught.

Homoglyph and zero-width characters blind keyword detection, while sequence and behavior detection hold.

Hold means no measurable drop, not good recall: behavior still misses most attacks, and the combined system drops under mutation. The numbers below say by how much.

Base64: the attack broke, it did not evade. 0/66 runs succeeded in each encoding, because this model could not act on the encoded text. There was no successful attack left to detect, so this is not an attacker win here. A model that can decode could change that.

Homoglyphs took content recall to 0/21, a change of -0.526. Zero-width characters took it to 1/23, a change of -0.519. Fullwidth characters did not move it, because the rules normalize them away.

Under homoglyphs alone, all tiers together fell from 16/19 to 9/21, a change of -0.414 [-0.683, -0.070], so 12 of 21 successful attacks got past every tier. Behavior's -0.098 [-0.167, +0.000] and sequence's -0.068 [-0.143, +0.000] do not exclude a real loss.

The result in four numbers

HeldBehavior -0.048 and sequence -0.005. Neither interval excludes zero.

Do not subtract across these. The 71 percent is all clean held-out attacks, while the pooled figures above use bases chosen because they succeeded, so they are different populations.

Individual rules are weak: pooled over clean or mutated attacks, no single rule clears the registered bar of 0.5 recall at 0.05 FPR, and the best is 7/28 = 0.250. Four rules caught nothing on clean attacks. The combination is what works.

How it works

  1. Attack

    Attack classes and benign work run against an instrumented agent in a sandbox.

  2. Telemetry

    Every run becomes schema-checked JSONL. Ground truth stays in separate labels.

  3. Detection

    A deterministic engine runs the rules. It never reads labels.

  4. Evasion

    Payloads are mutated by fixed code and re-executed against the live model.

  5. Measure

    Scores come from a frozen held-out split, each with numerator, denominator and interval.

What this does not show

  • One local model and one author. Results may not transfer.
  • All 20 succeeded held-out groups have a relative in the dev split, so clean recall may be inflated.
  • Small samples: 28 succeeded clean traces, and 38 benign traces that are 19 groups run in two guardrail arms. False positives were not re-measured under mutation.
  • Sequence rules depend on verbatim text overlap, so spelling can still reach them: at the longest overlap tested they score 0/21 on homoglyphs.
  • Beyond the 9 classes with successes, 3 attack classes never succeeded against this model, so detecting them is untested.
  • With guardrails on, mutated attacks succeeded in only 2/395 runs, too few to read a tier result from. The tier figures here are guardrails off.
  • Mutations are mechanical, not an adaptive attacker. Paraphrase and translation evasion were not measured.
  • Converted SIEM rules have not been run on any SIEM.
